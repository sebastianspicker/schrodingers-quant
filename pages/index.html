A breakout strategy, with the drawdown left in.

Schrödingers Quant is a Freqtrade spot bot for Kraken that ships in dry-run and starts stopped. Its strategy, H1, buys BTC/EUR when a 4h close breaks the 20-day high and sells when it breaks the 10-day low. This page shows the backtests that decided whether H1 could move on to paper trading, marked to market on every close, with fees, against simply buying and holding.

These are historical simulations on Binance prices used as a proxy for Kraken. They are not live results and they do not predict future returns. The held-out verdict is GO, marginal: enough to allow forward paper trading and a €10 execution test, nothing more.

The decision rule, written before the test

H1 was predeclared with fixed rules, fixed periods and four pass criteria. The held-out window was meant to be run once.

Why it's weak evidence

  • The held-out window was run twice. The first run compounded the stake against the declared rules and failed K2; the fixed-stake rerun is the one that counts, and both are published.
  • Fourteen held-out trades with a 36 % win rate. A few long trends carry the result.
  • The strategy family was chosen by someone who already knew how crypto prices moved.

So the next evidence has to come from data after 2026-09-24: forward paper trading on a VPS. Read the full experiment record.

How the bot is put together

Freqtrade is the only thing that places orders. Everything else checks, records or watches.

  • Ships inert. Tracked config is spot, dry-run, no credentials, and the bot starts in the stopped state, including after every restart and restore.
  • One order engine. Project code never calls an order-creating method; a test fails if it does.
  • Exits never wait on a model. The optional Jev filter can only block new entries, and it fails closed.
  • Built for a small box. Debian 13, 2 vCPU, 3 GB RAM, systemd timers, restic backups, a dead-man's switch for silence.
